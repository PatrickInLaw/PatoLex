# Reparse Plan — applying the cc019 parser fixes to the corpus

**Status:** DRAFT v1, pre-review · **Date:** 2026-07-25 · **Owner:** cc019

---

## What this is for

Four parser defects were fixed and corpus-verified in cc019. None of the fixes have been applied to the corpus — every artifact on disk was produced by the **pre-fix** parser (June 12). This plan gets the fixes into the corpus **without** silently invalidating the cc015–cc018 recovery campaign.

**Scope note:** this is **PARSING, not re-OCR.** No engine runs; no GPU is touched. The banked `consensus_text` is unchanged.

## The fixes being applied

| Defect | Effect | Measured exposure |
|---|---|---|
| 2 — em-dash / comma headings | recovers headings the regex could not match | +116 comma + 2 em-dash in 4 early volumes |
| 1 — unsigned / veto-override enactments | acts with no `[Approved …]` bracket now parse and date | 40 modern (1982–99) + 7/10 early-era |
| D — non-"An Act" headings | enacting clause accepted as act evidence | 1876 ch.508, 1870 ch.427 class |
| 3 — bracket ranges | residual manifest ranges widened + implausible-span flagged | partial; forward-scan still open |

## The core risk this plan exists to manage

The recovery chain — `fixed` → `merged` → `recovered` → `repaired` → `clauserec` → `visual` → `certified` — was built **from** the June-12 `parsed_acts_fixed.json`.

A reparse rewrites `fixed.json` **in place** and does **not** touch the downstream artifacts. **That is worse than clobbering them**: they become silently stale against their own input, with nothing flagging the mismatch. Everything would still look fine.

---

## Phase 0 — Establish the chain (BLOCKING; no writes)

**We do not currently know the recovery chain well enough to re-run it.** This phase is not optional and its output gates everything after it.

0.1 **Inventory the chain.** For every script that consumes or produces a `parsed_acts_*.json`: exact input(s), exact output(s), invocation, and required order. Sources: `pipeline/ingest/`, `pipeline/analysis/`, `pipeline/chapter/`, plus `BUILD_RUNBOOK.md`.

0.2 **Idempotency.** For each: is re-running it on the same input deterministic? Does it append, or replace? Does anything consume its own prior output (making it non-reproducible)?

0.3 **★ Irreplaceable-work audit.** Identify any artifact containing work that **cannot be regenerated by re-running code**:
- cc018's 12 VLM-recovered chapters (1907/1911/1953/1956)
- 1905 ch.389–397 (Google Books acquisition, `image_verified`, `textlen=0`)
- any human/manual correction
- anything in `parsed_acts_visual.json` sourced from a vision pass

**If re-running the chain would destroy any of this, the chain must NOT be re-run blind.** These need extraction and re-application, or the chain needs a merge step that preserves them.

0.4 **Snapshot.** Copy every `parsed_acts_*.json` across all 216 volumes to a timestamped tree (~2 GB; 542 GB free). Verify by count + total bytes. **Nothing proceeds without this.**

0.5 **Sync the 5090's repo.** It is at `ddce79e` (Jun 29) — 480 lines behind on `ingest_from_ocr.py` alone. Decide: `git pull` there, or continue staging files into a scratch tree.

**Exit criteria:** a written chain map, an explicit list of irreplaceable artifacts, a verified snapshot.

## Phase 1 — Diff, early era (no writes)

Run `reparse_diff.py --volumes 1865-66,1867-68,1869-70,1871-72,1873-74,1875-76,1877-78`.

This is where the comma and lapse fixes bite. Expect: chapters **gained**, dates **added**, zero lost.

**Abort condition:** any chapter LOST or date REMOVED → stop and investigate. The fixes are supposed to be strictly additive.

**Cost:** seconds.

---

## Phase 2 — Diff, full corpus (no writes)

`reparse_diff.py --all`. ~15–30 s at 16 workers.

Deliverable: total chapters gained/lost, dates added/changed/removed, enactment-path distribution, and per-volume detail for anything anomalous.

---

## Phase 3 — Review gate (Patrick)

Present the delta. **Nothing is written to the corpus before an explicit decision.** Specifically flag:
- any LOST chapter (regression)
- any CHANGED date (a date that moved is more alarming than one that appeared)
- volumes with unexpectedly large deltas

---

## Phase 4 — In-place reparse

Only after Phase 3 approval and Phase 0.4 snapshot.

`python -m ingest.parse_all` on the 5090 (~15–30 s). Verify: every in-scope volume has a fresh `fixed.json`; counts match the Phase 2 prediction exactly. **If they do not match, stop** — a mismatch means the diff was not measuring what the real run does.

---

## Phase 5 — Re-run the recovery chain

Per the Phase 0 map, in order, preserving the Phase 0.3 irreplaceable work.

**This is the phase with the most unknown cost.** The recovery passes did GPU/VLM work during cc015–cc018; if any of that must be redone, this is no longer a sub-minute job.

---

## Phase 6 — Re-measure

`chapter_vs_oracle.py` / `_recall_allyears.py` against the corrected corpus. Compare to the 99.9% (95,923/96,002) baseline. Re-measure the "51 acts wrong date" prerequisite, which is likely an undercount.

---

## Phase 7 — Fold in the contents recovery

The 71 recovered chapters live **only in a markdown table**. Convert to a machine-readable artifact and reconcile against what the corrected parser now finds independently — genuine cross-validation, since the two methods are independent.

---

## Rollback

Restore from the Phase 0.4 snapshot. Requires that snapshot to be verified before Phase 4.
