## 3. TASK B — Proposition presence check (HIGH VALUE, ~10 MIN)

**This is the highest-information-per-minute task of the day, and it is on no prior list.**

**The question:** Seven statewide elections have **no "Measures Submitted to Vote of Electors" section anywhere in the Chief Clerk digital series.** We cannot tell whether (a) the printed volumes never carried such a section, or (b) the Chief Clerk's scanning operation skipped it. **That distinction decides whether this is a scanning problem or a wrong-source problem** — and it is answerable in minutes with the physical book in hand.

Voter-approved initiatives amend statutes outside the "Chapter N" pipeline, so PatoLex currently **misses them entirely**. This is ~100 statute-affecting initiatives + ~54 constitutional changes, 1911→present.

**Pull these volumes and check the back matter + index of each:**

| Volume to pull | Would carry measures from |
|---|---|
| Statutes of California **1913** | Oct 10 1911 special election ⚠ *(the election that created the initiative power)*, Nov 5 1912 |
| Statutes of California **1917** | Nov 7 1916 |
| Statutes of California **1919** | Nov 5 1918 |
| Statutes of California **1921** | Nov 2 1920 |
| Statutes of California **1923** | Nov 7 1922 |
| Statutes of California **1925** | Nov 4 1924 |

**What to do for each:**
1. Check the **table of contents** and the **index** for any heading resembling "Measures Submitted to Vote of Electors," "Propositions Submitted…," "Referendum Measures," or "Constitutional Amendments Adopted."
2. Check the **back matter** — in the volumes that DO have it (1915, 1927+), this section sits after the chapters, before the index.
3. **If present → photograph the entire section.** This is the whole prize; it is authoritative enacted text we currently do not have in any form.
4. **If absent → photograph the table of contents and index pages** as negative evidence, and note it. That tells us to source these elections elsewhere and stop looking here.

> Reference for what "present" looks like: from 1927 onward these appear as separate `*_Measures.pdf` sections titled **"MEASURES SUBMITTED TO VOTE OF ELECTORS"** with `[Approved by electors DATE]` / "In effect DATE" brackets. The 1915 volume has a 2-page **"REFERENDUM MEASURES"** section — so a very short section is normal and easy to miss.

**Before the trip, if there's time,** check the UC Law SF (Hastings) California Ballot Propositions Database and HathiTrust for these years — a hit removes the need. But the physical presence check is free while you're there and is what tells us which avenue is even valid.
